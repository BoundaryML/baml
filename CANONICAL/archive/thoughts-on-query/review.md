# Review: the DataFusion query proposal vs the canonical design

**Status:** review only (no plan). Produced 2026-08-06 by an 11-agent research workflow: 3 grounding researchers (translation map with code verification, DataFusion capability research, gap analysis), 3 integration-shape evaluations, 4 adversarial verifiers attacking the load-bearing claims, 1 completeness critic. Claims marked [V] are version-sensitive knowledge, unverified against a pinned DataFusion release. The two files in this directory are byte-identical — one plan, reviewed once.

## Verdict in three sentences

The coworker independently reinvented roughly 60% of our shipped value plane and 40% of the SQL tier — on substrates we explicitly deleted (DataFusion + analytical SQLite + loose blob files) and without the four load-bearing concepts that make our design honest (grains, evidence states, structural tenancy, revision identity). **Fold in the ideas, not the engine**: four of its ideas are genuinely good and engine-neutral; every path that brings DataFusion itself in either resurrects the two-languages problem in a quieter form (DF-local), obligates us to build and operate a first-party multi-tenant lakehouse (DF-everywhere), or adds a ~400-crate dependency that no internal role actually needs (DF-internal). The strongest outcome of this review is a short harvest list plus one named, kept-warm exit (DF-everywhere, with triggers), and three real gaps this exercise exposed in **our own** docs.

## 1. What the plan actually is, translated into our nomenclature

| Their concept | Our equivalent | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `ValueId([u8;32])`, BLAKE3 content hash | CID (BLAKE3-256 over the canonical Merkle DAG, `bamlv_1_`) | Same instinct; different, **frozen** preimage |
| `values/<hex>.blob` one-file-per-value | BPK1 packs + `.bpki` index + bloom | Ours strictly stronger (dedupe measured ≥20×, crc32c, zstd, compaction, GC grace, upload pins); loose files = inode storm, no durability barrier, no seal/upload unit |
| "JSON-based first codec, binary later" | Frozen canon codec, golden-pinned bytes AND CIDs | **Two codecs = two CID spaces**: breaks dedupe, GC marks (`manifest.bamlcids`), `uploads.pin`, `GROUP BY cid`, verify-my-fix, hosted PRF tokens; their own "binary later" migration re-CIDs the entire store — the exact failure our new-domain-per-version rule prevents |
| `ValueHydrator` (recursion, dedupe, cycle detection, depth/size caps) | Budgeted decoder (`DecodeBudget`, elision markers carrying child CIDs) | Ours shipped; cycle detection unnecessary (Merkle DAGs are acyclic by construction); their truncation has no resumable handle |
| `function_calls` table | `value_roots_v1` ∪ `call_instances_v1` ∪ `cct_population_v1` | **The central hazard**: nothing in the plan pins the grain. Read as one-row-per-*captured*-call it is our value_roots grain (safe, rows ∝ captures); the name, the `status`/`metrics`/`metadata` columns, and the absent writer contract all pull toward one-row-per-*call* — the 38.5 TB/day disease the whole design exists to kill. `COUNT(*)` over it answers "how many *recorded*" while claiming "how many" — the exact silent-wrong failure the grain-named views + evidence ledgers prevent |
| `name TEXT` as identity; `cct_id TEXT` | `function_id` (dense u32/revision) + `definition_key` + `def_content_hash`; CCT node ids are session-epoch-scoped and re-keyed by every consumer | Their identity model silently merges recompiles, breaks on rename, and presumes stable global context ids that do not exist |
| Injected `project_id` predicate | Structural scoping: row policies bound to connection identity (hosted), filesystem (local) | Same intent; their mechanism (predicate injection, `WHERE project_id='p1'` in user SQL) is the spoofable variant our docs name and reject. Also: their shared blob dir dedupes across projects — cross-tenant CID equality is a confirmation oracle; our hosted CIDs are per-tenant PRF tokens for exactly this reason |
| NULL for missing values | 12-state availability vocabulary + non-overlapping precedence | Their design conflates no-args / capture-disabled / redacted / evicted / lost into one NULL — the precise "absent rendered as null" violation our invariant 8 forbids; and "invalid encoding → query failure" lets one corrupt blob poison a whole cohort answer |
| Pushdown classification, unsafe-OR rules, LIMIT-until-N, `HydrateExec` | — | **Artifacts of a split-engine design.** Under single-engine execution (one ClickHouse statement over projections) these problems dissolve; they are real only if you adopt their engine topology |
| `root.classify` UDF registry + result cache keyed (fn, version, model/config, input hash) | No equivalent | **The genuinely new ideas** — see harvest list. Note their own `deterministic: bool` flag concedes the problem: an LLM classifier is not deterministic, so the cache freezes a first-write-wins sample as a permanent verdict |
| QueryContext budgets, cancellation, allowlisted functions, read-only validation | DecodeBudget + `--max-bytes` (outside SQL); CONST/MAX profiles + quotas + `KILL QUERY` (hosted) | Convergent; ours are placed outside SQL so they survive a hostile statement |

Also missing entirely from the plan (it starts at the query engine and never looks down): who writes the rows and when; torn-tail recovery; crashed-run classification; sealed-artifact discipline; GC/retention/pins; near-live freshness; population aggregates of any kind (no histograms, no counters, no windows). None of this is a criticism of the coworker — the plan was written blind to our context — but it means the plan **as written** cannot be folded in; only translated.

## 2. Three ways to bring DataFusion in, each worked through

### Shape A — DF replaces clickhouse-local locally; hosted stays ClickHouse. **Rejected.**

What it genuinely buys: deletes the 0.5 GB engine download, closes the Windows gap (§17-6), deletes the 100–500 ms startup that is the local latency budget's dominant term (tens-of-ms in-process instead, [V]), and enables planner-integrated lazy hydration with row-granular early stop — the one capability class both our docs lack.

Why it loses — precisely, and not on the lazy "it's BQL again" line (our own verification layer refuted the equivalence: funded with exact-name shims and a symmetric gate, it is *less* severe than BQL on every axis the BQL decision recorded; unfunded, it is *worse*, because BQL couldn't silently disagree — it never looked like SQL):

1. **The enforce-or-diverge dilemma has no third option.** Enforce a common-subset gate on both planes → we forfeit "the full expressive surface" that justified SQL-everywhere (no combinators, no lambdas, ~1,500 ClickHouse functions outside the subset). Don't enforce → hosted-learned SQL fails locally and saved queries silently die at the local→hosted adoption boundary.
2. **Zero language maintenance dies and dialect authorship becomes ours.** "The ClickHouse SQL dialect" is one sentence of borrowed authority; under Shape A the contract becomes an intersection dialect we specify, shim (~8 functions incl. our own two UDFs — closable), analyzer-patch, gate, and document forever.
3. **The silent-semantics class is discovered, not enumerated.** Live demo from our own docs: the documented error-rate idiom `x / nullIf(y, 0)` parses and runs on both engines and returns **0 instead of 0.0037** on DataFusion (CH `/` → Float64; DF → truncating integer division) [K]. Known members are one analyzer rule each; the class boundary between two independent coercion matrices is found by fuzzing and incidents, never proven closed.
4. **The conformance corpus weakens in kind** — from a version-drift check between deployments of one engine to a semantics-equivalence proof between two independent engines — while §14 conditions the entire SQL decision on that corpus's strength.
5. **Engine count goes to three, not two.** The fold engine is irreplaceable for the UI workload, so Shape A yields fold + DF + CH, and the P0-A gate "local RPC, CLI, and SQL agree semantically" becomes a three-way proof.

### Shape B — DF everywhere: replace ClickHouse Cloud with a first-party DF tier over Parquet-on-S3. **Not v1. The named, kept-warm exit.**

This is the only DF shape that preserves the actual principle (one user-facing language) — a different language. It is coherent, and parts of it genuinely *simplify* the design: batch-identity-as-object-key + conditional create makes duplicates physically impossible and deletes the fiddliest CH machinery (§27.6 step-10 read-back with sequential consistency); projector/query isolation becomes free (the projector PUTs Parquet, never touches the query engine); scope-by-construction catalogs (tenant's files only) are structurally stronger than RLS *filters*; the enterprise story shrinks to PG + S3 + our images.

Why it is not v1, three load-bearing reasons (adversarially verified):

1. **Assurance downgrade at the worst spot.** Every §37.3/§37.4 control has a buildable DF analog, but tenancy drops from two-layer vendor-proven enforcement (our config + ClickHouse's decade-hardened policy engine) to single-layer first-party code whose audit meters are self-reported by the code being audited. A mis-provisioned CH identity fails closed (zero rows); a catalog-assembly bug fails *open* (someone else's files). The value-read column gate cannot be expressed as file partitioning at all (same objects, ±value-read identities) — it is a code-enforced mask forever. And no one in the DF deployment population (InfluxDB 3, GreptimeDB, Comet, dbt-fusion [V]) exposes DF as multi-tenant SQL passthrough; we would be first, on the security-critical axis, over a library with monthly breaking majors.
2. **An unpriced SLO hole.** Event→hosted-queryable p50 < 2 s with ≤1 s chunk age means queries must read a stream of KB–MB Parquet micro-objects before compaction — a storm of 20–80 ms S3 GETs. MergeTree's in-memory parts + merges + mark cache absorb exactly this; Shape B has no designed hot tier, and closing the hole means rebuilding the part of ClickHouse that does the most work.
3. **Scope and sequencing.** ~3–5× the hosted-analytics build of the locked plan (tenancy/metering wrapper, a bespoke table format or iceberg-rust/delta-rs adoption [V], a compaction fleet at admitted-rate×5, an NVMe cache tier, active-index re-homed to PG while remaining a §37.2 catalog view — an unresolved wart), to re-decide a days-old locked decision whose premise was *renting* exactly this machinery.

Two honesty corrections to the pro-B case: "the drift treadmill dies" is overclaimed — a fleet of user CLIs pinned at old DF versions × a continuously-redeployed hosted DF recreates pinned-vs-auto-upgrading skew with us as both vendors and no `compatibility` analog; and hosted users lose ClickHouse's full function surface unconditionally, not as a gate choice.

**If the exit is ever taken, take it before hosted GA** — the day users save CH-dialect SQL, the price doubles. Named triggers worth recording in §67: CH Cloud cost/dependency becomes untenable; the §56 cross-engine treadmill produces repeated errata; a hot-tier design exists; per-request scoped storage credentials are added as a second enforcement layer.

### Shape C — CH contract intact, DF as internal machinery. **Zero roles survive scrutiny.**

Examined role by role: the **deferred-scan executor** needs no relational engine — no SQL arrives (typed predicates), the data is trees under the frozen budgeted decoder, evidence accounting needs the 12-state vocabulary SQL NULLs destroy, and checkpointing below the SQS visibility ceiling means invoking DF as a very heavy per-chunk filter around code we'd write anyway. **Interactive lazy hydration** via DF either parses whole statements (dialect leak — the flagship catalog query fails on four counts under DF's function library) or reduces to a two-phase design where early-stop is a `take_while` in a hand loop. **Federation** is refuted twice over (below). **RAM-tap live SQL** would query revocable pre-durability bytes, violating the two-reader-visibility rule and adding a fourth semantic surface. **wasm** would ship a 4–8 MB gzip second bundle [V] carrying a third dialect to users who already have the first dialect server-side via per-session promptfiddle. With no kept role, the fixed cost (~350–450 transitive crates, 20–50 MB binary [V], 2–6 min cold builds [V], monthly-breaking API churn) is never amortized.

## 3. The federation question (your opening itch: "how people query local+cloud")

The adversarial verifier's finding, and I think it settles this: **the one join federation would uniquely enable is severed by our own cryptographic policy, and no engine can restore it.** Hosted CIDs are per-tenant keyed-PRF tokens minted *in the projector at projection time* — the serving views never contain raw CIDs, and every egress re-tokenizes. So a federated engine querying both planes can never join local raw CIDs against hosted tokens. That was a deliberate security decision (confirmation-oracle prevention), not a gap.

What actually restores the cross-plane verify-my-fix join is already in the design: **promote the local run** (the shipped ingest path re-tokenizes its CIDs under the tenant key; the PRF is deterministic per tenant+key-version, so promoted tokens equal prod tokens iff the raw CIDs were equal) — then it's one hosted CH statement under row policies. For everything else that legitimately crosses planes (population aggregates keyed by `definition_key` — small by construction), two queries + merge in the agent skill, or hosted-export + local `file()` join, cover it in one dialect with zero new engines. A story audit found **zero** single-statement cross-plane queries in either doc's 36 user stories; the genuinely cross-plane items are workflow verbs (promote, rerun, create-test) the design already names.

Cheap, real improvements here: ship `baml query --hosted [--both]` (route the same CH statement to the endpoint — missing from the command surface today, nearly free, and it *proves* the one-dialect promise at the seam), document the three cross-plane idioms as a schema-docs page, and hold the external-data user-attachment (size-capped local Parquet attached to a hosted statement — the price-table precedent generalized) as the P1/P2 extension if a demonstrated one-statement need ever appears.

## 4. The CANNOT matrix (what each choice forecloses)

**Under the locked design (CH everywhere) we cannot:**
- Run semantic/LLM evaluation *inside* a statement (mid-plan, dynamically-scoped, row-granular streaming early exit). The two-phase equivalent — classify distinct CIDs outside the query, join results back as external data — covers every documented user story with a *better* cost shape (LLM spend known before the first call) and is the sanctioned form; the residue is precisely "adaptive per-row classification steering other rows inside one plan," which is also banned independently of engine ("no LLM in the data plane").
- Do interactive full-body predicates with row-granular early stop; the loop form pays 100–500 ms engine startup per round (real gap, engine-neutral fixes exist — see harvest).
- Query with SQL fresher than ~1–2 s (deliberate: projections read at the durability horizon).
- Offer native-Windows, zero-download, sub-100 ms local SQL (the recorded price of dialect identity; if any of these becomes a hard product blocker, the dialect-preserving option to evaluate FIRST is an embedded ClickHouse — chDB-class — whose Rust-binding maturity is unverified [V], before any DF shape).
- One-statement local+cloud joins on value identity (severed by CID tokenization policy — restored only via promotion).
- Browser-local querying (accepted, server-backed promptfiddle is the replacement).

**Under Shape A we additionally cannot:** keep one meaning across planes without funding a permanent subset/shim/gate program; keep zero language maintenance; keep borrowed dialect authority; keep the corpus as a mere drift check; keep the engine count at two.

**Under Shape B we additionally cannot:** give hosted users ClickHouse's full function surface (unconditional loss); rent tenancy/budget enforcement (single-layer, self-audited, first-in-industry); meet the freshness SLO without building a hot tier; keep the active index inside the one-engine catalog story; avoid owning a lakehouse database team forever.

**Under the coworker plan as written we cannot:** answer any population question truthfully (no aggregate plane), distinguish absent from null (evidence states missing), survive recompiles (name-based identity), trust tenancy (predicate injection + cross-project CID oracle), keep one CID space (JSON codec forks it), or reconcile its store with GC/upload/retention (no barrier, no pins, no seal).

## 5. Harvest list — what folds in (all engine-neutral)

1. **Predicate-scoped hydration**: `baml query --hydrate --where <sql>` — a cheap CID/preview-tier pass computes the hydration scope instead of today's coarse `run=`/`role=` selectors. Small Q2 extension; captures most of HydrateExec's practical value.
2. **Close the G1 gap explicitly** — interactive early-stop body predicates. Either record it as an accepted loss in profiling §10.1's loss list, or spec an interactive small-scope mode of the §40 scanner with an early-stop-at-N contract (`baml playground scan` already reserves the slot). Caveat our own reviewers raised: whichever form we pick must specify how the 12 availability states render in a relational result — the NULL-conflation critique we leveled at the coworker applies to our own feature until written down.
3. **The CID-keyed classification dataset** (their best idea): results of expensive/semantic evaluation cached under (function, version, model/config version, input CID) and *joined into* SQL rather than executed inside it. Adopt as a P2 design **with its real bill attached**: it is a third artifact class (neither sealed evidence nor rebuildable projection — LLM output isn't recomputable) needing a GC/retention/deletion-cascade story; labels are value-derived columns → value-read gate applies; classification egresses customer bytes to a provider under §48 rules; LLM nondeterminism means the cache is a pinned sample, not a truth; and spend needs a budget axis §37.4 lacks (D6 territory).
4. **Cross-plane UX**: `--hosted`/`--both` + the documented idioms page (§3 above).
5. **Their tests 1–6, 11–13 into the Q2/Q3 corpus** (zero-blob-reads for resident-only queries; per-role hydration isolation; distinct-CID-once; availability-not-NULL; cancellation stops all stages — and note local `baml query` cancellation is currently unstated in our docs, hosted-only).
6. If Shape B is ever taken: native-Rust quantile UDAFs sharing fold-engine code (strengthens bit-identity from convention to shared code).

## 6. What this review exposed in OUR design (independent of DataFusion)

1. **No reader-concurrency contract.** GC's locking protects *writers*; pack compaction swaps files under readers; projection compaction unlinks per-run Parquet; retention can tombstone sources under an open scan. POSIX tolerates the unlink-under-read; Windows does not [K]. A minutes-long scan or a resident server holding handles across a swap has no stated contract today — this needs a paragraph in profiling §8.6 regardless of engine.
2. **Resident-engine visibility semantics unstated.** The locked local SQL model is per-invocation (natural snapshot; hot tail regenerated per invocation). Any resident execution (in-app SQL box §17-1, promptfiddle session servers) needs a manifest-keyed invalidation contract we haven't written.
3. **The download's incidence was never priced.** Laptop-once-cached is a weak objection; per-fresh-CI-runner and container-image cost is the strong one; promptfiddle is server-side/amortized. Worth one honest paragraph in §10.4 so the next re-litigation starts from facts.
4. **The G1 hole and the classification-lane absence** (harvest items 2–3) are genuine unrecorded gaps, not DF artifacts.

## Bottom line

Folding the *plan* in: no — it re-derives shipped machinery on weaker substrates and violates seven non-negotiables it couldn't have known about. Folding *DataFusion* in: not now, in any shape — A loses on the design's own recorded terms once the refuted "=BQL" framing is replaced by the real legs (enforce-or-diverge, dialect authorship, corpus weakening, live silent-wrong demo); B is coherent but is a database-team charter re-deciding a fresh lock, kept warm as the named exit with triggers; C keeps zero roles. Folding the *ideas* in: yes — five concrete harvests, four of them cheap, plus three real fixes to our own docs that this exercise surfaced. The coworker's instincts about hydration, budgets, and content addressing were right — right enough that the shipped design already contains their stronger forms.
